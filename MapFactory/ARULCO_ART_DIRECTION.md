# Arulco Map Factory Art Direction

## Setting anchor

Arulco is a fictional, impoverished Latin-American country in the late-1990s JA2 setting. Its economy was distorted away from self-sufficient agriculture toward intensive extraction/export of natural resources under Deidranna. Public services and social investment were gutted while the regime, mines, airports, SAM sites and military infrastructure remained strategically important.

The visual target is therefore not a generic jungle, desert, Caribbean resort or modern developed state. It is a poor, climatically varied Latin-American country with a strong contrast between civilian colour and institutional decay.

## Core visual rule

POOR DOES NOT MEAN COLOURLESS.

Low-cost owner-built settlements should mix:
- faded turquoise, blue, pink, yellow, green and terracotta plaster;
- exposed brick, concrete block and patched stucco;
- rusted corrugated metal and mismatched roof sheets;
- painted doors, shutters, curbs and hand-painted commercial signs;
- improvised timber fences, wire, patched walls and scavenged materials;
- laundry, awnings, market tarps, barrels, crates, old vehicles and domestic clutter;
- dense tropical plants where climate supports them.

Colour must be weathered: sun bleaching, dirt, damp staining, rust, soot and chipped paint prevent a resort-like look.

## Material hierarchy

Civilian:
- colourful but worn plaster;
- brick / block / timber repairs;
- zinc and corrugated roofs;
- mixed paving, dirt, mud and broken concrete;
- small gardens and productive vegetation;
- improvised extensions and irregular lot boundaries.

Agricultural:
- saturated crop greens;
- dark wet soil, red-brown / ochre earth;
- irrigation, drainage, field edges and muddy tracks;
- sheds, crates, tools, drums, pallets, fences and farm junk;
- visible differences between planted, mature, harvested and neglected fields.

Mining / industrial:
- rust orange, oxidised steel, faded yellow hazard paint, dirty teal/blue machinery;
- black oil stains, grey concrete, mineral dust and scrap;
- strong industrial geometry but cheap maintenance;
- worker shacks / service buildings much rougher and more colourful than core plant.

Military / regime:
- functional concrete, sandbags, wire, prefabs and corrugated metal;
- more standardised than civilian areas but still a poor-country army;
- faded olive, khaki, dirty white, oxidised metal, hand-painted markings;
- checkpoints and bases should look maintained enough to function, not NATO-clean.

Wilderness:
- climate-specific rather than universally tropical;
- humid zones: deep saturated green, muddy soil, water, vines and dense growth;
- plains: dry yellow-green grass, red-brown dirt and scrub;
- hills: exposed rock, dry vegetation, erosion and rough tracks;
- swamps/coast: strong water colour, reeds, dark wet ground and patched settlements.

## Settlement character

Omerta / poorest war-damaged areas:
- bomb damage, abandoned houses, broken public infrastructure;
- very cheap materials;
- faded civilian colour surviving among ruin.

Oronegro:
- coastal / tropical working town language;
- colourful low-income housing and commercial facades;
- industrial/oil infrastructure nearby should contrast sharply with civilian areas.

Salinas:
- declined regional town;
- old hospital/public-service fabric visibly neglected;
- more urban colour than wilderness sectors, but cracked, patched and underfunded.

Drassen:
- working transport/mining town;
- worker housing, warehouses, airport/service buildings;
- practical streets, loading areas and industrial clutter.

Military/SAM sectors:
- regime investment concentrated into security infrastructure;
- still visibly an under-resourced authoritarian army rather than a rich Western military.

## Colour target

At normal tactical zoom, each sector must contain clearly separable colour families:
1. vegetation,
2. soil/ground,
3. buildings,
4. roofs/metal,
5. roads/hardstanding,
6. local accent colour.

A remaster that is technically different but still reads as one brown/grey/green mass fails visual QA.

## Production rule

The Map Factory may alter:
- palette/material art direction,
- terrain dressing,
- vegetation density,
- clutter,
- reusable composition kits,
- cosmetic building surfaces,
- non-gameplay visual props.

It should preserve unless explicitly redesigned:
- collision,
- doors and locks,
- quest triggers,
- NPC placements,
- exits,
- tactical chokepoints,
- scripted structures,
- map logic.

The objective is a visibly richer, more colourful, hand-authored Arulco that still looks poor, politically neglected and believable.
